## Answers to the 17 decision questions

1. **Enough demand?** Probably yes for a small business, but unproven. Local printed bakery boxes and stock boxes are sold (Atlas, Box House) [Low–Medium], and the plastic-bag ban favours paper bags [Medium]. No count of Valley prospects was verified. The Base case needs about 44 paying customers in 12 months.
2. **Best entry segment?** Skincare, soap, candle and personal-care brands, then bakeries and boutiques (see `04_customer_prospect_database.csv`).
3. **Launch products (3–5)?** Printed corrugated mailer/bakery boxes, branded kraft paper bags, folding cartons, rigid gift boxes (few, high-value), stickers/labels as an add-on.
4. **Best repeat, cost and margin mix?** Folding cartons and rigid boxes have the highest contribution per order (about NPR 5,000–8,000). Bags and bakery boxes bring repeat. Stickers have the weakest outsourced economics under the assumed costs.
5. **Full workshop realistic within NPR 5 lakh?** **No.** Config C totals NPR 7.5 lakh, and one cutter alone costs 37–61% of the budget before duty.
6. **Exact machines?** **None** at launch. Hand tools only (about NPR 30,000). After the gate: a sticker/label cutter (advertised ₹1.15–1.9 lakh, about NPR 1.84–3.04 lakh) only if label orders justify it and a written landed quote exists.
7. **Outsource?** All printing, die-cutting, lamination, gluing and rigid-box making.
8. **Minimum capital to launch safely?** NPR 1,50,000 for the pilot (Config A). NPR 5,00,000 for the full plan, with a peak need of about NPR 4.75 lakh (founder unpaid).
9. **Monthly fixed cost?** NPR 32,500, or NPR 47,500 once NPR 15,000 founder pay starts.
10. **Break-even?** About NPR 1.73 lakh/month sales = 6 orders at an average NPR 28,242 (NPR 2.53 lakh = 9 orders with founder pay). About 34 active customers at an 18% monthly reorder rate would carry 6 orders on reorders alone.
11. **When?** Base case: operating break-even in month 7 (before founder pay), month 9 (after).
12. **Cash in the first six months?** Base-case net cash outflow from operations is about NPR 1.87 lakh, plus NPR 2.65 lakh of set-up spend, so about NPR 4.5 lakh of the 5 lakh is committed by month 6 under the full plan.
13. **Strongest advantage?** Small-run, design-included, sample-first service with one accountable contact. This is an inference and has not been customer-tested.
14. **Assumptions that matter most?** Supplier cost (gross margin 24.8%), new-customer ramp, founder pay, collection days.
15. **Verify before buying machinery?** Landed quote including customs and VAT, service availability in Nepal, consumable costs, power needs, and 3 months of label-order data showing demand.
16. **Expand in-house when?** Label/sticker revenue of at least NPR 1,00,000 a month for 3 months running and a landed machine cost recovered within 12–18 months from the outsourced margin saved (assumption).
17. **Abandon or change when?** See stop-loss rules below.

## Quantified conditions for "go" at day 90
- At least 4 paying customers and at least NPR 90,000 billed.
- Contribution of at least NPR 3,000 on each order and blended gross margin of at least 25%.
- Written quotes from at least 2 suppliers per launch product.
- Deposits collected on every order and no overdue balances older than 15 days.
- A Nepal CA's written confirmation of the registration and VAT route.

## Stop-loss rules
1. At Day 90: fewer than 2 paying customers, or less than NPR 50,000 billed, or gross margin below 15% → **stop**. Maximum loss about NPR 1.5 lakh or less.
2. After release of Stage 2: if cash falls below NPR 1,00,000 while monthly contribution is below the fixed cost of NPR 32,500 for 2 consecutive months → **freeze all spending and review**.
3. If cash falls below NPR 50,000 (the contingency level) → **stop new commitments** and wind down or seek other income.
4. Never take founder pay until two consecutive months of positive operating profit.

## One-page decision memo (to a founder who cannot afford to lose this money)

**Recommendation:** Start, but only small. Spend at most **NPR 1,50,000** in the first 90 days. Do not buy a machine. Do not rent a workshop.

**Why start at all.** Local printed packaging for bakeries exists and sells. The plastic-bag ban helps paper. A one-person design-and-sourcing business needs no machinery and its losses are capped if you stop early. In the Base case it breaks even on operations in month 7 and has positive cash if you take no salary.

**Why not commit all NPR 5 lakh.** Under reasonable assumptions the margin is thin (about 25% gross, about 19% after delivery and rework). Break-even needs about 6 orders a month of about NPR 28,000 each. If customers come at half the pace, or suppliers charge 10% more, the business runs out of cash inside the NPR 5 lakh. Four of nine stress tests drain more than NPR 1 lakh beyond the cash you have even when you take no salary, and the Downside case burns the entire budget.

**What I could not verify.** Supplier prices, customer demand, the number of businesses you can reach, and Nepal's rules on VAT classification and machinery duty. Everything depends on getting real quotes and real orders.

**What to do in the next 14 days.**
1. Meet a CA and confirm registration and the VAT route.
2. Build a verified list of 150 prospects and interview 5.
3. Send the RFQ in `06_supplier_quotation_request.md` to 3 suppliers per product.
4. Make samples for 10 prospects and ask for a deposit-backed first order.

**Walk away if:** quotes cut your gross margin below 25%, or you cannot get 4 paying customers by Day 90. **Scale up only if:** margins hold, customers reorder, and you have been paid on time.

**Do not** take a salary before month 7 at the earliest, do not give credit to new customers, and do not buy equipment until the label business justifies it in numbers.
