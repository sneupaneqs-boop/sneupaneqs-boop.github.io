## 4. Customer pain points (hypotheses to test, not facts)

Large MOQs, high small-batch prices, limited customisation, poor print consistency, no structural design, no prototypes before bulk orders, long lead times, and the burden of coordinating design, print, finish and delivery. **No interview or public review evidence was found.** Test with 5 interviews in week 1 using these questions: what do you buy, from whom, minimum quantity, price per piece, lead time, last bad experience, would you pay for design and samples.

**Funnel (Base, assumption):** about 80 contacts a month → about 15% qualified → about half request a quote → part convert to a first paid order, giving about 3.7 new customers a month on average. Over 12 months that needs roughly 900 contacts and so a verified prospect list of that size.

## 6. Competitive landscape

See `03_competitor_database.csv` (14 candidates; only The Box House Nepal was fetched in full; **fewer than 15 verified competitors**). Observations:

- **Local box makers** (Box House in Bhaktapur, Atlas in Gairidhara, Global Packaging in Mahalaxmi, Shree Ganapati in Jadibuti) compete on corrugated, bakery and pizza boxes. Box House shows retail stock prices of NPR 10–35 per box, which anchor customer price expectations for plain boxes.
- **Industrial printers** (Chandika Print Pack) serve large FMCG clients, not small brands.
- **General job printers** (API Printing Press) do cards, flex and stationery.
- **Handmade-paper exporters** (Nepali Paper Products, Mahaguthi, Shubham Handicrafts, Himalayan Paper Works) cover gift bags and boxes with a handcrafted look.
- **Indian suppliers** (Step Design Printways, S. R. Art Printers) quote for Nepal and compete on rigid boxes and stickers.
- No Kathmandu rigid-box specialist, labels specialist or paper-bag print factory was found. This may be a real gap or a search-tool gap.

**Where a new entrant can compete (ranked by customer value, competition, difficulty, margin):**
1. Small minimum quantities with a paper sample before print (high value, medium competition, medium difficulty).
2. One-stop design plus procurement (high value, low-medium competition).
3. Custom dimensions with structural design (high value, medium competition).
4. Transparent, itemised quotations (medium value, low difficulty).
5. Recurring supply plans (medium value, builds retention).
Fast delivery and low price are not recommended. They depend on suppliers and on capital the startup does not have.

**Positioning statement:** *"We make your branded packaging easy to order in small runs: design in your size, a paper sample before you pay for print, one clear quote, and delivery to your door."*

## 7. Business models compared

Weighted scores from the workbook (`Model_Comparison` sheet; 15 criteria, weights sum to 100, scores 1–5 are analyst judgements):

| Model | Capital | Machinery | Weighted score |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Design and sourcing agency | NPR 2.65 lakh set-up + reserves | None | **3.85** |
| B. Hybrid design + light conversion | Adds a label/sticker cutter, NPR 1.84–3.8 lakh (advertised to landed estimate) | One machine | 3.18 |
| C. In-house workshop | Several machines plus stock and labour; breaches the NPR 5 lakh ceiling with a safe reserve | Many | 1.86 |

A wins on capital, product range, break-even and downside risk. It loses on production control. B adds control over short runs and prototyping, but the machine's advertised cost alone is 37–61% of the budget. C is rejected: Config C in the workbook totals NPR 7.5 lakh.

**What to keep in-house:** design, hand-made white mock-ups, quoting, supplier QC, delivery and invoicing. **What to outsource:** printing, die-cutting, lamination, gluing, rigid-box making.

## 9. Pricing and unit economics

Full table in `07_product_pricing_model.csv`. All supplier costs and prices are **assumptions pending RFQ**. Minimum-viable quantity is the smallest tier with contribution of at least NPR 3,000 per order.

| Product | Minimum viable tier | Revenue at that tier | Gross margin | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kraft paper bag | 500 | 23,875 | 23% | 4,597 |
| Folding carton | 100 (die and design fees charged) | 28,350 | 21% | 4,940 |
| Printed corrugated mailer | 250 | 29,850 | 25% | 6,222 |
| Sticker/label 50 mm | 5,000 | 20,575 | 34% | 6,206 |
| Rigid gift box | 50 | 42,050 | 22% | 7,989 |
| Bakery/takeaway box | 500 | 24,750 | 24% | 4,919 |

Under these assumptions, outsourced stickers lose money below about 5,000 pieces. That is the one result that supports buying a cutter later, if real quotes confirm it.

**Pricing architecture:** unit price by quantity tier; separate setup/die charge at 1.15× the supplier's charge; design fee on custom structures; paper sample fee credited on order; 50% deposit; minimum contribution of NPR 3,000 per order; delivery charged separately below an order value; rush +20–30%; VAT shown separately.
