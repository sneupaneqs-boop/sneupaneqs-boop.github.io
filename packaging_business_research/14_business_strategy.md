# Business Strategy: What to Do and How (Kathmandu Valley Custom Packaging)

Date **2026-10-23**. Companion model: `15_strategy_model.xlsx` (all numbers below come from it). Audit basis: `13_audit_report.md`. Evidence grades: **V** fetched page, **S** search snippet or secondary source, **A** assumption.

## 0. The strategy in one paragraph

Don't try to be a small packaging factory, and don't be a reseller with a markup. Be **Kathmandu's small-run packaging studio**. Small brands should be able to get branded packaging in **days, from 100 pieces, at a fixed published price**. The method is to copy what makes Indian small-run players work (Printo, EzPac, Diva Design), adapted to Nepal:
1. **Sell a ladder of offers.** Start with a cheap stock-box-plus-custom-sticker kit and move customers up to fully printed packaging.
2. **Route each job to the cheapest source.** Indian digital printers, pooled monthly, take short runs. Nepal factories take bulk runs, bags and rigid boxes.
3. **Cut fixed and variable costs to the bone.** Work from a home office, use 3PL delivery at NPR 80, gang-run stickers, and keep a library of standard sizes.
4. **Grow through partners.** Use logistics companies' merchant bases, not cold outreach alone.

**Result:** in the model's base case, contribution margin rises from 20% to about 31%. Monthly fixed cost falls from NPR 32,500 to NPR 18,000–27,000. The business stays solvent within NPR 5 lakh in the base, conservative and stress cases, and fails only in a severe case (§8).

### India: the small-business playbook to copy

| Lesson | Who | Evidence | How we apply it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stock box + custom sticker = branded look without dies or plates** | Printo "sticker-embellished mailer", ₹33 from 25 pieces | V | Our **T1 Brand Starter Kit** |
| **Fixed SKUs + instant online price** remove quoting and sales cost | EzPac live price calculator; Printo per-SKU pricing | V | Published price card plus a simple website calculator |
| **Digital printing kills the 500–1,000 MOQ** | EzPac from 10 pieces; digital short runs of 50–100 have no die | V | T2 short runs via digital, never offset below about 250 |
| **"Start with branded labels on plain packaging"; standard sizes; one vendor; off-peak buying (Jan–Mar)** | onlineprintout.in small-business guide | V | Standard-size library; off-peak stock buys |
| **No-minimum label press built against the 500-MOQ norm** | Diva Design, Ahmedabad | S | Gang-run sticker batching with one local digital printer |
| **Sourcing platform connecting brands to factories** | Bizongo | S | Our T3 broker role, for retention only (margin about 9%) |
| **Express tier at a premium** | Printo same-day in selected cities | V | Rush fee +25% |

**Indian price ceiling (landed in Kathmandu, consolidated, unverified duty 15%):** a mono carton or mailer from Printo is about **NPR 90/unit**; a rigid box about NPR 629; a logo kraft bag NPR 28–45. A customer who can import from India pays about the same, plus the hassle of doing it. Our short-run prices must stay at or below this ceiling plus a convenience premium.

## 2. Where the demand is (anchors found in the audit)
- More than 90% of Nepal's online merchants are in the Kathmandu Valley. Nepal Can Move has 1,000+ merchants; Pathao Commerce signed 600+ merchants in two weeks (Kathmandu Post, 27 Aug 2026, S).
- 1,530 e-commerce firms applied for registration under the E-commerce Act; 345 approved (S).
- About 10,000 online SMEs nationally (self-reported, MIT Solve, S).
- 170,632 establishments in the Valley (2018 Economic Census, all sectors, S).
- Social commerce reportedly grew 30–40% a year over five years (S).

**Implication:** the densest, most reachable customer pool is the **Valley's online sellers**. They ship parcels every day, need presentable boxes, and are reachable through a handful of logistics partners. Bakeries and skincare/candle brands remain the second and third segments.

## 3. What to sell: the offer ladder

From the Offer_Ladder sheet; prices are ex-VAT.

| Tier | Offer | Example order | Price | GM | Contribution per order | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Brand Starter Kit** | Stock box/bag + custom gang-run sticker + tissue; 3–5 days | 100 kits | NPR 80/kit + 1,500 setup = **9,500** | **44%** | 3,791 | **Entry offer.** Low ticket, fast, high margin, frequent reorders |
| **T2 Short-run Custom** | Fully printed cartons/mailers, 100–300 pieces, digital (India pooled or local digital) | 200 cartons | NPR 140 + 2,000 design = **30,000** | **37%** | 9,815 | **Profit engine** |
| **T3 Bulk Custom** | 1,000+ offset from Nepal factories; die kept for reorders | 1,000 mailers | NPR 52 + 4,600 setup = **56,600** | 13% | 5,313 | Retention for graduated customers only |
| **T4 Design-only** | Packaging and label design, dieline, mock-up | 1 package | **8,000** | **85%** | 6,440 | Cash-positive from day 1, zero inventory |
| **T5 Subscription** | Monthly scheduled delivery from bulk stock | 300 units/month | **21,000/month** | 30% | 5,529 | Locks in recurring revenue |

**Selling motion:** win the customer with T1 or T4, move them to T2 once they need fully printed boxes, offer T5 to the top 10%, and use T3 only when volume exceeds about 1,000. **Postpone:** flexible pouches, pharma, and food-contact boxes without written food-grade confirmation.

## 4. Where to make it: sourcing rules
From the Sourcing_Decision sheet; Nepal costs are assumptions until quoted.

| Job | Cheapest route (model) | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Printed carton or mailer, **100–250 pieces** | **India digital, consolidated** (about NPR 90 vs Nepal offset about NPR 106–199 including the die) | Pool all customers' short runs into **one monthly shipment** of about 1,000 units. A single-customer shipment costs NPR 111–153/unit and fails. |
| Printed carton or mailer, **1,000+** | Nepal factory (NPR 42–47) | Keep the die; offer reorders |
| Kraft bags | Nepal (NPR 27–36 vs India NPR 45 landed) | Bulky and cheap; don't import |
| Rigid gift boxes | Nepal (NPR 343–453 vs India NPR 629) | Find the supplier first; India is the backup |
| Stickers and labels | Local digital printer, **gang-run** | Don't import; don't buy a printer (see §5) |

**Precondition:** before the first import, get a clearing agent's written duty quote for HS 4819 and an Indian supplier invoice that states GST/export treatment. At 30% duty and NPR 15,000 fixed cost per shipment (scenario S2), T2 margins fall but stay positive.

**Not to do:** buy a label printer. An Epson ColorWorks C4050 plus cutter costs about NPR 5.5 lakh landed (unverified). At NPR 2.1 saved per label and about 8,300 labels a year, payback is about 31 years (Label_Printer_BE sheet). Revisit only above about 15,000 labels a month.

**VAT trade-off (C6):** registering removes 7.8% cost leakage (scenario S4: profit +NPR 49,000). But it adds 13% to prices for unregistered customers, who are most Instagram sellers. Register voluntarily only if at least half of revenue comes from VAT-registered businesses, or when turnover nears NPR 30 lakh.

**Pricing architecture:**
1. Published price card per SKU and quantity tier (100 / 250 / 500 / 1,000).
2. Setup/die passed through at 1.15×.
3. Design fee credited against the first print order.
4. Sample fee credited against the order.

6. 5% off for a 6-month subscription.
7. Minimum contribution NPR 3,000 per order.

## 8. What the model says (Strategy_Scenarios sheet)

| Scenario | Customers | Revenue | CM % | Op. profit after founder pay | Min operating cash | Solvent ≤ 5L? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 08 v2 Base (old strategy) | 44 | 19.8 lakh | 20.3% | −0.78 lakh | −0.22 lakh | No |
| **S0 Strategy base** (+50% customers) | 66 | 23.3 lakh | 30.9% | **+3.44 lakh** | +2.04 lakh | **Yes** |
| **S1 Conservative** (same customers as old Base) | 44 | 15.6 lakh | 30.9% | **+1.03 lakh** | +1.89 lakh | **Yes** |
| S2 Stress (duty 30%, prices −10–12%, slower reorders) | 44 | 12.9 lakh | 21.0% | −1.07 lakh | +0.71 lakh | Yes |
| S3 Severe (S2 + 30% fewer customers + founder NPR 25k from month 4) | 31 | 9.0 lakh | 21.0% | −3.24 lakh | −1.28 lakh | **No** |
| S4 Base + VAT registered | 66 | 22.6 lakh | 34.1% | +3.93 lakh | +2.10 lakh | Yes |

Break-even (S0): fixed cost NPR 27,000/month ÷ 30.9% contribution = **about NPR 87,000/month of sales, about 5–6 orders**. With NPR 15,000 founder pay: about NPR 1.36 lakh/month, or 8 orders.

**Why the strategy beats the old plan even with the same number of customers (S1):** fixed costs are about NPR 1 lakh a year lower, and contribution margin is about 11 points higher. The higher margin comes from the high-margin T1 kits, India-pooled short runs, design packages, and NPR 80 delivery. The lower AOV (NPR 16,600 vs 26,700) is deliberate: smaller tickets close faster and reorder more often.

**What would break it:** S3. That means demand at 70% of the old plan, Indian duty at 30%, prices pushed down, and the founder drawing NPR 25,000 from month 4. Guard against it with the stop-loss rules in `12_final_investment_recommendation.md`.

## 10. Risks specific to this strategy

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Indian import duty or GST higher than assumed; customs delays | Verify before the first shipment. Keep a Nepal digital printer as fallback for T2. Price T2 off the *verified* landed cost. |
| Stock-box supplier (e.g., Box House) sees our kits as competition | Buy from 2–3 stock suppliers. Our value is the brand layer, design and service. |
| Partner channel doesn't convert | Partners are upside (R3), not in the base case. Keep direct outreach. |
| Low-ticket T1 orders consume founder time | Fixed SKUs, templates, prepayment, and a minimum order of 50 kits |
| Direct Model-A competitors (PFA, Packaging Product) copy the price card | Speed (3–5-day T1), local sampling, and reorder service build switching costs |
| Food-contact orders | Written food-grade confirmation from the supplier before any bakery or takeaway order |

**Decision:** still **conditional go**, now with a better offer design. Run the 90-day pilot with the T1/T4 entry offers and quote-verified T2 sourcing. Scale only if the Day-90 gate is met.
